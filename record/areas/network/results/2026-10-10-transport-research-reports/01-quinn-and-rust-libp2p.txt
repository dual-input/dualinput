### Datagrams (RFC 9221)
- **API:** all on `Connection`: `send_datagram(Bytes) -> Result<(), SendDatagramError>`, `send_datagram_wait(Bytes)`, `read_datagram()`, `max_datagram_size() -> Option<usize>`, and `datagram_send_buffer_space()`. [DOC] https://docs.rs/quinn/0.11.12/quinn/struct.Connection.html
- **Errors:** `UnsupportedByPeer`, `Disabled`, `TooLarge`, and `Blocked(Bytes)`. [SRC] quinn-proto 0.11.19, src/connection/datagrams.rs, `SendDatagramError`
- **Size formula:** max size = min(peer's `max_datagram_frame_size` − 9, current MTU − (1 + remote CID length + 4 + 16) − 9). [SRC] `Datagrams::max_size` (datagrams.rs L73-96), `predict_1rtt_overhead` (connection/mod.rs L3757-3776), `Datagram::SIZE_BOUND = 1 + 8` (frame.rs L902)
- **Size with defaults:** an 8-byte CID, a 1,200 B starting MTU, and MTU discovery up to 1,452 B give 1,162 B at the start and up to 1,414 B later. [INFERENCE] from the formula, the `TransportConfig` defaults, and `MtuDiscoveryConfig::upper_bound` "Defaults to 1452" [DOC]
  - The docs promise the limit is "a little over a kilobyte at minimum" when the peer's limit is large. [DOC]
  - quinn advertises `max_datagram_frame_size = min(datagram_receive_buffer_size, 65535)`. [SRC] transport_parameters.rs L170-172
  - Our ≤1 KB traffic batches fit.
- **When congested, quinn queues datagrams and drops the oldest.**
  - `send_datagram` pushes onto one FIFO, `datagram_send_buffer_size`, which defaults to 1 MiB. When the FIFO is full, `make_space_for` pops the oldest unsent datagrams ("dropping outgoing datagram"). [SRC] datagrams.rs L19-56 and L154-161
  - The docs say: "Previously queued datagrams which are still unsent may be discarded … oldest to newest." [DOC]
  - `send_datagram_wait` waits instead, "which effectively prioritizes old datagrams over new datagrams." [DOC]
  - The receive buffer defaults to 1,250,000 B and drops the oldest when full ("dropping stale datagram"). [SRC] datagrams.rs L145-148; config/transport.rs `impl Default`
  - The API has no per-datagram acknowledgment or loss notification. [DOC] Connection method list
  - RFC 9221 §5.4 allows a sender either to "delay sending the frame until the controller allows it or drop the frame." [DOC]
- **Datagrams are congestion-controlled.** They are ack-eliciting (RFC 9221 §5.2). `poll_transmit` blocks an ack-eliciting packet when `in_flight.bytes + bytes_to_send >= congestion.window()` ("blocked by congestion control"), and it also paces ("blocked by pacing"). [SRC] connection/mod.rs L604-638

### Streams and priority
- **Priority API:** `SendStream::set_priority(i32) -> Result<(), ClosedStream>`. Every stream starts at 0, and higher-priority data is sent first. The docs add: "Changing the priority of a stream with pending data may only take effect after that data has been transmitted." [DOC] https://docs.rs/quinn/0.11.12/quinn/struct.SendStream.html
- **Scheduler:** `write_stream_frames` pops "the stream of the highest priority that currently has pending data", so priority is strict. [SRC] quinn-proto 0.11.19, connection/streams/state.rs L598-654
  - `send_fairness` (default `true`) round-robins streams of equal priority. [SRC] transport.rs default
- **Between datagrams and streams there is no setting.** `populate_packet` writes every queued DATAGRAM frame ("// DATAGRAM", L3350-3365) before STREAM frames ("// STREAM", L3406-3412) in each 1-RTT packet. [SRC] connection/mod.rs
  - So queued datagrams always go ahead of all stream data, bulk included.
  - Datagrams among themselves are one FIFO with no priority, although RFC 9221 §5.1 says implementations "SHOULD present an API" for that. [SRC] [DOC]
- **What priority can't control:** it orders only what goes into the next packet. Bulk data already in flight still fills the congestion window and the router's queue, and a datagram waits for ACKs to free window space. So bulk must be rate-limited by us, through app-level pacing or a small `send_window`. [INFERENCE]
- **Independent streams:** each stream is reliable and ordered, and a loss on one stream doesn't delay another. This is a QUIC property.

### Congestion control
- **Choices:** Cubic is the default. NewReno is available. `Bbr` is labeled "Experimental! Use at your own risk." A custom controller can be plugged in through `ControllerFactory`. [DOC] https://docs.rs/quinn-proto/0.11.19/quinn_proto/congestion/index.html; [SRC] transport.rs default `CubicConfig`
- **Datagrams wait, they aren't starved.** The congestion window and the pacer hold them back. They are dropped only when the send FIFO overflows. [SRC] as above
- **Worst case for our traffic:**
  - Cubic's minimum window is `2 * self.current_mtu`, and persistent congestion resets the window to it. [SRC] congestion/cubic.rs L88-90, L201-214
  - At a 100ms round-trip time, 2 × 1,200 B per round trip is about 24 KB/s, above our ~5 KB/s per pilot.
  - So congestion control alone shouldn't starve the v1 channels. Bulk sharing the window is the risk. [INFERENCE]

### Migration and paths
- **RFC 9000:**
  - "Only clients are able to migrate in this version of QUIC" (§1).
  - "Migrating a connection to a new server address mid-connection is not supported" (§9.6).
  - On NAT rebinding, the receiver "MUST initiate path validation" (§9.3).
  - [DOC] https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9000.html
- **Server side:** `ServerConfig::migration` defaults to true ("Improves behavior for clients that move … or suffer NAT rebinding"). [SRC] config/mod.rs L244, L288-291
  - `migrate()` resets congestion and RTT state unless the new address has the same IPv4 address. [SRC] connection/mod.rs L3063-3103
- **Client side:** the client drops packets from an unknown remote ("discarding packet from unrecognized peer"). [SRC] mod.rs L1128-1132
- **Active migration:** `Endpoint::rebind` and `rebind_abstract` switch the local socket "affecting all active connections." [DOC] https://docs.rs/quinn/0.11.12/quinn/struct.Endpoint.html
  - Internally this sends `ConnectionEvent::Rebind`. [SRC] quinn 0.11.12, endpoint.rs L253-270
- **Peer-to-peer consequence:** the peer that plays QUIC server can't change its address. If that peer's NAT rebinds its port, the client discards the packets and the connection dies at the idle timeout. [INFERENCE] from the SRC above
- **No multipath upstream.** The README doesn't list it [DOC], and `TransportConfig` on main has no multipath field [SRC].
- **`path_changed` exists but is hard to reach.**
  - quinn-proto has `Connection::path_changed(now)`, which "Resets path-specific settings": the congestion controller, RTT estimator, pacer, and MTU discovery. [DOC] https://docs.rs/quinn-proto/0.11.19/quinn_proto/struct.Connection.html
  - quinn's high-level `Connection` doesn't expose it. Its 28 methods include no `path_changed` and no `ping`. [DOC]
  - Calling it means driving quinn-proto ourselves. quinn-proto "contains no networking code." [DOC]
- **Relay ↔ direct inside one connection: not native.**
  - The only route is a custom `AsyncUdpSocket` that shows quinn one stable virtual peer address and routes each `Transmit` direct or via the relay.
  - That was iroh's design before noq ("QUIC does not know the underlying network paths used").
  - iroh found the stale congestion state took about 20s to settle. After their fork reset the controller's state, it took about 3s. [MEASURED-BY-OTHERS] https://www.iroh.computer/blog/why-we-forked-quinn (2024-05-20)
- **noq:** n0 then hard-forked quinn as noq, which carries the multipath draft, so the relay and direct links become QUIC paths of one connection. [DOC] https://iroh.computer/blog/noq-announcement (2026-03-19)
  - noq 1.3.0 was published 2026-09-09 under MIT/Apache-2.0. [DOC] https://docs.rs/crate/noq/latest
  - It's a separate candidate, probably in the iroh agent's group.

### One socket for hole punching
- **Socket hooks:** `Endpoint::new` takes a `std::net::UdpSocket`. `new_with_abstract_socket` takes an `Arc<dyn AsyncUdpSocket>`, whose methods are `create_io_poller`, `try_send(&Transmit)`, `poll_recv`, `local_addr`, and others. [DOC] https://docs.rs/quinn/0.11.12/quinn/trait.AsyncUdpSocket.html
- **Sending punch packets:** libp2p-quic calls `socket.try_clone()` before handing the socket to quinn. On the clone it sends 64 random bytes at random 10–200ms intervals (`rand::random_range(10..=200)`). [SRC] rust-libp2p master, transports/quic/src/transport.rs (`listen_on`, `dial`), hole_punching.rs
- **Receiving non-QUIC packets** such as STUN replies on that socket needs a wrapping `AsyncUdpSocket`, because quinn's receive loop reads everything. [INFERENCE]
  - If STUN shares the socket, set `EndpointConfig::grease_quic_bit(false)`. It defaults to `true`. [SRC] config/mod.rs L63
  - draft-seemann-quic-nat-traversal says the QUIC bit can't be greased in that setup. [DOC] I saw this in a search snippet of draft -02 and didn't read the draft in full.
- **QUIC has no simultaneous open.** DCUtR's QUIC pattern: A dials on `Sync`, and B "starts to send UDP packets filled with random bytes", "between 10 and 200 ms". The result is A as client and B as server. [DOC] https://github.com/libp2p/specs/blob/master/relay/DCUtR.md
- **Retry timing:** quinn's `initial_rtt` defaults to 333ms [SRC], which gives "handshakes starting with a PTO of 1 second", doubling after each expiry [DOC] RFC 9002 §6.2.1-6.2.2.
  - So an Initial dropped by the peer's NAT is retried after about 1s unless we lower `initial_rtt`, for example from the RTT measured through the relay. [INFERENCE]
- **Windows:** quinn's receive loop ignores `ConnectionReset` ("Ignore ECONNRESET as it's undefined in QUIC"). [SRC] quinn 0.11.12, endpoint.rs L843-846
  - So ICMP port-unreachable replies during punching shouldn't kill the endpoint, assuming Rust maps WSAECONNRESET to `ConnectionReset`. [INFERENCE]
- **Examples and crates:**
  - libp2p-quic, described above.
  - noq/iroh, which have it built in.
  - quinn's own examples have no hole punching: client, server, connection, insecure_connection, single_socket. [SRC] repo listing

### Bytes per packet and handshake
- **Header pieces:**
  - The short header is 1 flags byte + the destination CID + a 1–4 byte packet number. [DOC] RFC 9000 §17.3.1
  - The AEAD tag is 16 bytes. [DOC] RFC 9001 §5.3
  - quinn's default CID is 8 bytes (`HashedConnectionIdGenerator`). [SRC] cid_generator.rs L167-168; config/mod.rs L57-61
  - A DATAGRAM frame is a type byte (0x30/0x31) plus a length varint, and quinn always writes the length (`encode(true, …)`). [SRC] frame.rs L906-913; datagrams.rs L217
- **For a 140 B flight-model sample:**
  - QUIC adds 1 + 8 + 1–2 + 16 + 1 + 2 = 29–30 B.
  - With IPv4/UDP, that's 57–58 B a packet. With IPv6/UDP, it's 77–78 B. [INFERENCE]
- **ACKs:** a receiver "SHOULD send an ACK frame after receiving at least two ack-eliciting packets" (§13.2.2), and `max_ack_delay` defaults to 25ms (§18.2). [DOC] RFC 9000
  - When both directions carry data, the ACKs ride along in data packets. [INFERENCE]
- **Handshake:**
  - The client can send application data after 1 round trip. [DOC] RFC 9001 §2.1
  - 0-RTT works only when resuming a session (`Connecting::into_0rtt`), and the server can send 0.5-RTT data. [DOC]
  - Retry is the application's choice and costs one more round trip if used. [DOC] `Incoming::retry`
  - quinn pads client Initial datagrams to at least `min_mtu` (1,200 B). [DOC] transport.rs
  - QUIC's anti-amplification limit (RFC 9000 §8.1) caps the server's first flight, so certificate size matters. [DOC] I didn't re-read §8.1 here.
- **Self-signed certificates:**
  - The examples generate them with `rcgen::generate_simple_self_signed`.
  - `insecure_connection.rs` skips verification with `.dangerous().with_custom_certificate_verifier(SkipServerVerification::new())`.
  - [SRC] quinn main, quinn/examples/common/mod.rs and insecure_connection.rs
  - Pinning a fingerprint exchanged through session signaling would be the better verifier. [INFERENCE]
- **Encryption off:** quinn has no switch.
  - The third-party `quinn-plaintext` 0.3.0 (2025-08-27, "Plaintext quinn cryptography (insecure)", depends on quinn-proto ^0.11.8) replaces the AEAD with an 8-byte SeaHash checksum (`tag_len` 8) and drops header protection (`sample_size` 0). [SRC] quinn-plaintext 0.3.0, src/lib.rs
  - It saves only 8 B a packet, and QUIC still needs its 1-round-trip Initial/Handshake exchange for transport parameters. It isn't worth it. [INFERENCE]

### Keepalive and idle timeout
- `keep_alive_interval` defaults to `None`: "Only one side of any given connection needs keep-alive." [DOC/SRC] transport.rs
- `max_idle_timeout` defaults to 30s ("30 second default recommended by RFC 9308 § 3.2"). The effective timeout is the minimum of both peers' values. [DOC/SRC] transport.rs

- **DCUtR:**
  - Peers exchange `Connect` and `Sync` over the relay to measure RTT, then run the QUIC punching pattern above.
  - The spec says to retry twice, for three attempts. On failure the peers "can keep using the relay connection." [DOC] DCUtR spec
  - The direct connection is a new connection, and long-lived streams have to be reopened on it rather than migrating.
  - Our record holds a measurement: 70% ± 7.1% of hole punches connected directly, 97.6% of those on the first try. [MEASURED-BY-OTHERS] Trautwein et al., cited in Crosscheck `record/areas/network/log.md`
- **Circuit relay v2:**
  - It works over any transport. The relayed connection flows in a `hop` stream over whatever connection the peer has with the relay, TCP or QUIC, and the endpoints then "upgrade the relayed connection with a security protocol and a multiplexer." [DOC] relay/circuit-v2.md
  - rust-libp2p does `.upgrade(V1Lazy).authenticate(..).multiplex(..)`. [SRC] libp2p/src/builder/phase/relay.rs `with_relay_client`
  - So the relayed path is one reliable byte stream with no datagrams, and every channel can stall behind a loss on any other. [INFERENCE]
  - Default limits: `max_circuit_duration` 2 min, `max_circuit_bytes: 1 << 17` (128 KiB), `max_circuits` 16 (4 per peer), and a 1h reservation. [SRC] protocols/relay/src/behaviour.rs `Config::default`
  - At ~5 KB/s, 128 KiB lasts about 26s. The limits are settings on a relay we host. [INFERENCE]
- **It isn't competitive for us.** It has no unreliable channel, no priorities, and no path switching within a connection, and its relayed path is head-of-line blocked. [INFERENCE]
  - What's worth taking from it is DCUtR's timing method and its measured success rate.

## Verdict

**quinn alone covers the transport layer.**
- One reliable ordered stream per channel, where a loss on one stream doesn't delay another.
- Strict stream priority.
- Datagrams that always go ahead of stream data, with drop-oldest when the queue overflows.
- Cubic congestion control that our rates fit under even at the minimum window.
- Two endpoints per PC.
- A 1-round-trip handshake.
- About 30 B of QUIC overhead per packet.

**What we'd still build:**
1. **Newest-only scheduling per channel.** quinn's datagram queue is one FIFO shared by all channels. We'd keep a one-slot mailbox per unreliable channel, and either shrink `datagram_send_buffer_size` or hand a datagram to quinn only when `datagram_send_buffer_space()` has room. [INFERENCE]
2. **Bulk pacing,** so bulk data in flight never fills the window or the router's queue ahead of the flight model. [INFERENCE]
3. **Hole punching:**
   - Gathering candidates (STUN or the address the relay sees), including IPv6.
   - Router port mapping (UPnP/PCP), which quinn lacks.
   - Signaling through the relay.
   - A DCUtR-style timed dial plus a punch spray on the same socket, with a socket wrapper for STUN.
4. **A UDP relay** that we write and host. quinn has none.
5. **Switching paths mid-session and duplicating packets.** quinn can't move a connection between a relay address and a direct one. There are three ways around it:
   - (a) A virtual-address `AsyncUdpSocket` that switches or duplicates below QUIC.
     - quinn discards duplicate packets through its "RFC4303-style sliding window" (`Dedup`, spaces.rs L459-521). [SRC]
     - This also hides the NAT-rebinding gap of the server-role peer.
     - The catch is stale congestion and RTT estimates unless we drive quinn-proto and call `path_changed`.
   - (b) Two QUIC connections, one direct and one relayed, with app-level sequence numbers for datagrams and app-level resending for reliable channels when we switch.
   - (c) noq, which does relay-as-path natively through multipath but on a draft spec.

**Net:** quinn gives us roughly the whole transport and none of the connectivity layer. That connectivity layer is about the scope of iroh's MagicSock. [INFERENCE]

**Still unknown, unsettled by this read:**
- quinn's behavior measured on Windows.
- Whether the quinn client follows a server's `preferred_address`.
- Whether quinn accepts zero-length CIDs. They would save 8 B a packet, but RFC 9000 §5.1 warns they "will cause failures in the presence of peer connection migration, NAT rebinding, and client port reuse."
