### 1. webrtc-rs (`webrtc`) and its sans-IO core (`rtc`)

**Versions and status**
- `webrtc` 0.21.0 and `rtc` 0.21.0 were both published 2026-09-19 [DOC, crates.io API `/crates/webrtc`, `/crates/rtc`].
- The new architecture is very young. `webrtc` 0.20.0 came out 2026-07-31, and 0.21 went through alpha, beta, and rc between 2026-08-09 and 2026-09-04 [DOC, crates.io].
- The README calls 0.21.x "the recommended choice today". `webrtc` is "a thin async layer over `rtc`" [DOC, github.com/webrtc-rs/webrtc README].
- The runtime is chosen by feature: `runtime-tokio` (the default) or `runtime-smol` [DOC, same README].
- The old tokio-coupled line ended at v0.17. The project calls it "the final feature release of the Tokio-coupled implementation", with bug fixes "through 2026" [DOC, webrtc.rs/blog/2026/01/31/async-friendly-webrtc-architecture.html]. Its last release was 0.17.2 on 2026-07-20 [DOC, crates.io].
- The 1.0 gating issue #836 was closed 2026-08-22 with every item done [DOC, github.com/webrtc-rs/webrtc/issues/836].
- One author does almost all the work: `rtc` contributors are rainliu with 601 commits, and the next contributor has 23 [DOC, GitHub contributors API].

**Known problems**
- **Relayed sessions drop on a stale nonce (`rtc` #263, open).** The allocation Refresh isn't retried after `438 Stale Nonce`, so relayed connections drop after about 65 minutes [MEASURED-BY-OTHERS, github.com/webrtc-rs/rtc/issues/263].
- **Relayed sessions drop on a ChannelBind refresh (`rtc` #267, open).** A ChannelBind refresh that gets a 438 deletes the binding, and rebinding is rejected with 400. Against an unnamed production TURN server, sessions dropped about 10m40s after Allocate [MEASURED-BY-OTHERS, issue #267]. The maintainer replied "please submit a PR" on 2026-10-04.
- **64 KB message cap (`rtc` #252, open)** [DOC].
- **TURN over TCP is post-1.0 (`webrtc` #848)** [DOC].
- **DTLS 1.3 is post-1.0 (`rtc` #149, `webrtc` #801)** [DOC].

**ICE**
- Full ICE, with `lite` as an option [SRC, `rtc-ice/src/agent/agent_config.rs`].
- ICE restart exists: `pub fn restart(now, ufrag, pwd, keep_local_candidates)` [SRC, `rtc-ice/src/agent/mod.rs`]. There is also an `examples/ice-restart` [DOC, rtc repo].
- Nomination is regular nomination [SRC, `rtc-ice/src/agent/agent_selector.rs`].
- Once a pair is selected it never changes. Both selection points are guarded by `selected_pair_is_none` [SRC, same file].
- On failure the agent runs `set_selected_pair(now, None)` and `delete_all_candidates(false)`, so it doesn't fall over to another pair [SRC, `rtc-ice/src/agent/mod.rs`].
- Keepalives go only to the selected pair (`check_keepalive`, RFC 7675 consent) [SRC, same file].
- There is no renomination and no continual gathering [SRC].
- Defaults: disconnected after 5s, failed after 25s [SRC, `agent_config.rs`].

**TURN client** (`webrtc/src/peer_connection/transport/turn_relayer.rs`) [SRC]
- It allocates, refreshes, creates permissions, and rotates credentials.
- Transport is UDP only. It logs "Skipping unsupported secure TURN url" for `turns:` and "Skipping unsupported non-UDP TURN url" for `?transport=tcp`.
- `rtc-turn` (`client/relay.rs`) binds a channel lazily on the first permitted send, and that first send goes out as a Send indication. It refreshes permissions every 120s and the allocation at half its lifetime [SRC].

**Data channel options** (`src/data_channel/init.rs`) [SRC]
- `RTCDataChannelInit` has `ordered`, `max_packet_life_time`, `max_retransmits`, `protocol`, and `negotiated: Option<StreamId>`. It has no priority field.
- `rtc-datachannel` sets a `priority: u16`, but only copies it into the DCEP open message (constants 128, 256, 512, and 1024) [SRC, `rtc-datachannel/src/data_channel/mod.rs`, `message_channel_open.rs`].
- Sends are unbounded by default. `try_send` fails with `ErrSendBufferFull` only once a limit is configured [SRC, `webrtc/src/data_channel/mod.rs`].

**SCTP scheduling** (`rtc-sctp/src/queue/pending_queue.rs`) [SRC]
- There is no per-stream priority. The queue has two FIFOs, `unordered_queue` and `ordered_queue`, and unordered always goes first.
- The fragments of a message are sent back to back. While a fragmented message is "selected", the other queue waits.
- There is no I-DATA (RFC 8260 interleaving). `chunk_payload_data.rs` has `PAYLOAD_DATA_HEADER_SIZE: usize = 12` plus the 4-byte chunk header, and no I-DATA chunk exists.

**Windows**
- CI builds on `windows-latest`, but the test matrix is `os: [ 'ubuntu-latest' ] #, 'macos-latest', 'windows-latest' ]` [SRC, `.github/workflows/cargo.yml` in both `webrtc` and `rtc`].
- `rtc` needs Rust edition 2024 and uses `ring` by default [DOC, rtc README].

### 2. str0m

**Versions and maintainers**
- 0.24.1 was published 2026-10-03. Releases have come roughly monthly through 2026 [DOC, crates.io].
- MSRV is 1.85.0 [SRC, `Cargo.toml`].
- Several people maintain it: algesten (1149 commits), k0nserv (156), davibe (133), thomaseizinger (110), xnorpx (95), and pthatcher (30) [DOC, GitHub contributors API].
- The README says it was "originally developed by Martin Algesten of Lookback".

**Sans-IO model**
- "the `Rtc` instance itself is not doing any network talking". It "has no internal threads or async tasks" and "is fully sync" [DOC, README].
- You drive it with `poll_output` (Transmit, Timeout, or Event) and feed packets back as `Input`. This drops into a tokio task that owns the sockets [DOC, docs.rs/str0m].
- `DirectApi` skips SDP entirely: `set_remote_ice_credentials`, `set_remote_fingerprint`, `start_dtls`, `start_sctp`, `start_sctp_with_snap`, `create_data_channel`, and `invalidate_candidate` [DOC, docs.rs `change::DirectApi`].

**ICE**
- Full ICE, with `set_ice_lite` as an option [DOC, docs.rs RtcConfig].
- The ICE agent ships as its own crate, `is` 0.11.1 (2026-09-25) [DOC, crates.io].
- "We let the gathering and evaluation of candidate pairs continue regardless of nomination state" and "The ICE agent can change the nominated pair without needing an ICE restart" [DOC, `docs/ice.md`].
- In the code, `handle_timeout` schedules binding attempts on every pair, and each pair's next attempt is capped at `now + max_rto()`. `max_rto` defaults to 3000ms, with 9 retransmits [SRC, `crates/is/src/agent.rs`, `crates/is/src/pair.rs`].
- So succeeded direct and relayed pairs both stay checked. A failed nominated pair is pruned, and `evaluate_nomination()` picks the next-best succeeded pair [SRC].
- Trickle ICE is required ("We do not support ICE _without_ trickle ice"). Local candidates can be added at any time [DOC, `docs/ice.md`].

**TURN**
- Not included. The feature table marks Turn ❌ [DOC, README].
- You call `Candidate::relayed(addr, local, proto)` with an allocation you made yourself: "Allocate a TURN addr to use as a local candidate" [DOC, docs.rs Candidate].
- Open issue #723 asks for STUN/TURN support [DOC].

**Data channels**
- `ChannelConfig` has `label`, `ordered`, `reliability` (`Reliable`, `MaxPacketLifetime { lifetime: u16 }`, or `MaxRetransmits { retransmits: u16 }`), `negotiated: Option<u16>`, and `protocol`. It has no priority [DOC, docs.rs].
- `Channel::write` returns `Ok(false)` when the shared buffer is full. `set_sctp_max_buffered_amount` defaults to 128 KiB, summed over all channels [SRC/DOC].
- Max message size is 256 KiB locally and defaults to 64 KiB for the remote [SRC, `src/sctp/mod.rs`]. Open issue #844 says "max-message-size is not passed to sctp-proto".

**SCTP scheduling**
- str0m uses `sctp-proto` 0.10.5, which the str0m maintainers have maintained since January 2026 [DOC, github.com/algesten/sctp-proto].
- It has the same two-FIFO design as webrtc-rs: unordered first, no per-stream priority, no I-DATA [SRC, `sctp-proto/src/queue/pending_queue.rs`].
- Open issue #977 evaluates moving to `dcsctp`.

**DTLS**
- `dimpl` 0.7.5 does DTLS 1.2 and 1.3 [DOC, crates.io, dimpl README].
- `set_dtls_version` takes `Dtls12` (the default), `Dtls13`, or `Auto` [DOC, docs.rs].

**SNAP**
- `set_snap_enabled` / `start_sctp_with_snap` implement draft-hancke-tsvwg-snap. Both sides exchange their SCTP INIT out of band, which skips SCTP's 4-way handshake [SRC, `src/sctp/snap.rs`].
- The draft is an expired individual draft (-00) [DOC, datatracker].

**Windows**
- CI runs the tests on `windows-latest` with the `aws-lc-rs`, `rust-crypto`, `wincrypto`, and `wincrypto-dimpl` features [SRC, `.github/workflows/cargo.yml`].

**Worth knowing: `dcsctp`**
- `dcsctp` 0.1.15 (2026-10-01, github.com/webrtc/dcsctp) is a Rust SCTP. It describes itself as "a spiritual successor to the C++ version" in libwebrtc.
- Its API has `enable_message_interleaving` (RFC 8260), `set_stream_priority`, and per-stream `buffered_amount`, and it is sans-IO (`handle_input`, `advance_time`, `poll_event`) [DOC, docs.rs dcsctp::api].

### 3. Bytes, round trips, encryption, and message size

**Encryption can't be turned off**
- RFC 8831 §6.1: "The DTLS encapsulation of SCTP packets as described in [RFC8261] MUST be used" [DOC].
- Neither stack offers a NULL cipher. `rtc-dtls` lists only AEAD and CBC suites, and dimpl says "(no CBC/EtM modes)" [SRC/DOC].
- str0m's `set_fingerprint_verification(false)` drops authentication, not encryption [DOC].

**DTLS bytes per record**
- DTLS 1.2 header is 13 B (RFC 6347 §4.1).
  - AES-GCM adds an 8 B explicit nonce and a 16 B tag (RFC 5288 §3), for **37 B**. This is the webrtc-rs default first choice [SRC, `rtc-dtls/src/cipher_suite/mod.rs`].
  - ChaCha20-Poly1305 has no explicit nonce and a 16 B tag (RFC 7905), for **29 B** [DOC].
- DTLS 1.3 unified header (RFC 9147 §4) is 1 flags byte, a 1–2 B sequence number, and an optional 2 B length. Add the 1 B inner content type and the 16 B tag for **about 19–22 B** [DOC + INFERENCE].
- str0m budgets `MAX_DTLS_OVERHEAD: usize = 48` as its worst case [SRC, `src/io/mod.rs`].

**SCTP bytes**
- Common header: 12 B (RFC 9260 §3.1).
- DATA chunk header: 16 B. I-DATA is 20 B, "4 bytes more than the length of the DATA chunk header" (RFC 8260 §2.1).
- Chunks pad to 4 B.
- SACK is at least 16 B. The receiver SACKs "at least every second packet" and "within 200 ms" (RFC 9260 §3.3.4, §6.2) [DOC].
- SACKs mostly ride along with data, since both pilots send [INFERENCE].

**Per-message total**
- One 140 B flight-model sample in one packet, direct IPv4, DTLS 1.2 GCM: 20 + 8 + 37 + 12 + 16 = **93 B of overhead**, about 233 B on the wire [INFERENCE from the above].
- Of that, WebRTC adds about 65 B over bare UDP.
- TURN adds 4 B on the client-to-server leg with ChannelData. A Send indication adds about 36 B: STUN header 20 + XOR-PEER-ADDRESS 12 + DATA 4 (RFC 8656 §11.1, §12.4) [DOC + INFERENCE].

**Handshake round trips**
- **ICE:** 1–2 RTT, counting the check plus the USE-CANDIDATE nomination [INFERENCE, RFC 8445].
- **DTLS 1.2:** 2 RTT, plus 1 with a HelloVerifyRequest. RFC 6347 §4.2.1 says the cookie exchange "SHOULD" be the default [DOC]. Whether either crate sends a HelloVerifyRequest is [UNVERIFIED].
- **DTLS 1.3:** 1 RTT, plus 1 with a HelloRetryRequest cookie [DOC, RFC 9147 §5.1].
- **SCTP:** INIT/INIT-ACK, then COOKIE-ECHO, which "MAY be bundled with any pending outbound DATA chunks". First data arrives at about 1.5 RTT (RFC 9260 §5.1) [DOC].
- **DCEP:** 0 extra RTT. The sender "MAY start sending ... without waiting for ... DATA_CHANNEL_ACK" but must send ordered until then (RFC 8832 §6) [DOC]. Negotiated channels skip DCEP.
- **Totals after signaling:** about 4.5–7 RTT with webrtc-rs. About 2–4 RTT with str0m using DTLS 1.3, SNAP, and negotiated channels [INFERENCE].
- Our server adds the signaling exchange on top.

**Message size and fragmentation**
- SCTP fragments a message larger than about MTU − 48 − 40 B into several chunks [SRC, str0m `src/sctp/mod.rs`].
- With no interleaving, RFC 8831 §6.6 says "the sender SHOULD limit the maximum message size to 16 KB to avoid monopolization" [DOC].
- RFC 8831 §6.4 says priorities "MUST be interpreted as weighted-fair-queuing scheduling priorities" [DOC]. Neither Rust stack does this.

### 4. Cloudflare Realtime TURN

**Credentials**
- Our server POSTs to `https://rtc.live.cloudflare.com/v1/turn/keys/$TURN_KEY_ID/credentials/generate-ice-servers` with `Authorization: Bearer $TURN_KEY_API_TOKEN` and `{"ttl": 86400}`. It returns `iceServers` (HTTP 201) [DOC, /realtime/turn/generate-credentials/].
- "You should keep your TURN key on the server side". Credentials can be revoked [DOC].
- A credential can expire "up to 48 hours in the future" [DOC, FAQ].

**Transports and ports**
- UDP on 3478 (alternate 443/udp), TCP on 3478 (alternate 80), TLS on 5349 (alternate 443) [DOC, /realtime/turn/].
- A custom-domain CNAME works for UDP and TCP, but not TLS [DOC, /realtime/turn/custom-domains].
- "Realtime does not implement RFC6062", so it relays UDP only [DOC, FAQ].
- Relay addresses are IPv4 only: it "will not respect `REQUESTED-ADDRESS-FAMILY`" [DOC, FAQ].
- It "denies CreatePermission or ChannelBind requests if private IP ranges" [DOC, FAQ].

**Pricing**
- "$0.05 per GB", "based on the data sent from the Cloudflare edge to the TURN client", including all TURN overhead [DOC, FAQ].
- The FAQ's billing diagram shows ingress free and server-to-peer traffic unbilled [DOC, FAQ].
- "The first 1,000 GB each month is free", and "SFU and TURN share this allowance" [DOC, /realtime/pricing/].

**Use from a plain TURN client, outside WebRTC**
- No Cloudflare doc restricts which clients may use it.
- Its explainer says "You can use TURN directly in your application too" and names "webrtc-rs in Rust" [DOC, /realtime/turn/what-is-turn/].
- Someone else confirmed it works. cloudflare-docs issue #33972 (2026-10-04, open, no Cloudflare reply) used its own client and aioice 0.10.2, with plain UDP sockets as peers [MEASURED-BY-OTHERS].
- **The same report found a cap:** an allocation takes data from at most 10 peer addresses over its whole life. The 11th "get[s] nothing (no Data indication, no error)", and its ChannelBind gets a 400 [MEASURED-BY-OTHERS].
- That matters to us because ICE checks our relay candidate against every remote candidate address [INFERENCE].
- Per-allocation limits: more than 5 new IPs per second, about 5–10 kpps, about 50–100 Mbps; "Hitting these limits will result in packet drops" [DOC, /realtime/turn/].

**Anycast latency**
- An allocation is "homed in the nearest available Cloudflare data center to the TURN client" [DOC, FAQ].
- Packets sent to the relayed address "enter the Cloudflare network as close to the sender as possible" and are forwarded "over our backbone to the correct datacenter" [DOC, blog.cloudflare.com/webrtc-turn-using-anycast, 2024-09-25].
- When both ends use Cloudflare TURN, it can "control the routing". The FAQ says the latency gain varies by location, and the loss reduction is the more predictable gain [DOC].
- So a relayed path doesn't detour through a fixed server far from either pilot. Whether it's near-optimal is unmeasured [INFERENCE].

**Incident**
- "performance degradation affecting our TURN service since August 22nd": traffic was relayed over TCP instead of UDP. It was fixed 2026-09-07, but "existing connections" stayed on TCP until they restarted [DOC, Cloudflare status via pulsetic.com/status/cloudflare/incidents/7021].

### 6. Switching paths mid-session

**What standard ICE allows**
- RFC 8445 §8.1.1: "MUST NOT nominate another pair ... within the ICE session". Changing the pair "requires an ICE restart" [DOC].
- After selection, "keepalives are only sent on those pairs" (§11) [DOC].
- Renomination (draft-thatcher-ice-renomination) expired in 2017 [DOC, datatracker].
- Continual gathering is a libwebrtc policy (`GATHER_CONTINUALLY`), not a standard [DOC, webrtc.googlesource.com].

**webrtc-rs**
- You only get ICE restart through renegotiation over our signaling server.
- The relay pair doesn't stay warm. The allocation is still refreshed, and `restart(..., keep_local_candidates)` can reuse it [SRC + INFERENCE].
- Expect a gap of about 5–25s to detect the failure, plus a signaling round trip [SRC defaults + INFERENCE].
- Whether DTLS and SCTP survive an ICE restart in `rtc` is [UNVERIFIED].

**str0m**
- Both the direct and the relayed pair stay checked at least every `max_rto`, which defaults to 3s.
- Nomination moves without a restart, and DTLS and SCTP sit above ICE, so they don't notice the switch [SRC/DOC].
- How long it takes to declare the direct pair dead is set by `set_max_stun_rto` and `set_max_stun_retransmits`, and is unmeasured.
- Switching back once the direct path recovers may need its candidates re-added, because failed pairs are pruned [INFERENCE, UNVERIFIED].

### Verdict

**How close it gets.** WebRTC data channels cover most of the requirements:
- per-channel ordered or unordered delivery
- `maxRetransmits` and `maxPacketLifeTime`
- independent stream ordering, so one channel's loss doesn't hold back another's delivery
- ICE with STUN and TURN

They fall short in three places:
- **No priority scheduling in either Rust stack.** Every ordered message from every channel shares one FIFO, and a large message monopolizes it. We would schedule above SCTP, keep the SCTP buffer shallow with `write`→`Ok(false)` and the buffered-amount events, and cut bulk into messages of about 1 KB or less.
- **Mandatory DTLS plus SCTP costs bytes.** About 65 B per packet with DTLS 1.2, or about 50 B with DTLS 1.3.
- **Standard ICE can't switch paths without a restart.**

**Crate.** str0m fits best:
- it's sans-IO under our own tokio loop
- full ICE keeps checking every pair and switches the nominated pair without a restart, so both paths stay warm
- `DirectApi` avoids SDP
- it has DTLS 1.3 and SNAP
- its CI runs the tests on Windows
- several people maintain it

webrtc-rs 0.21 works, but:
- its architecture is about 10 weeks old, and one author does almost all the work
- TURN is UDP only, with two open bugs that drop relayed sessions
- the selected pair never switches
- CI builds on Windows but doesn't run the tests there

**What we'd still build** on str0m:
- signaling through our server: ICE credentials, candidates, DTLS fingerprint, and the SNAP INIT
- candidate gathering: host candidates including IPv6, server-reflexive via STUN, and port-mapped addresses
- a TURN client: credentials minted by our server; allocate, refresh, permissions, ChannelBind, and stale-nonce handling. `rtc-turn` is sans-IO but has the open bugs above.
- socket I/O and demultiplexing, with relay packets wrapped and unwrapped as ChannelData
- the priority scheduler
- a forced-relay dev switch that adds only the relay candidate. On one PC through Cloudflare, both cores must run relay-only, because Cloudflare refuses permissions to private addresses [INFERENCE].

**Cost per relayed session-hour.** These are estimates [INFERENCE], assuming 5,000 B/s of payload per pilot, about 50 packets/s, and about 71 B of TURN+DTLS+SCTP overhead per packet, plus SACKs and STUN:

| Case | Billed traffic | Cloudflare TURN | Self-hosted coturn |
|---|---|---|---|
| One pilot relayed | about 31–37 MB/h | about $0.0017/h | $4–6/month flat |
| Both pilots relayed | about 64–74 MB/h | about $0.0035/h | $4–6/month flat |

- Cloudflare's free 1,000 GB/month covers about 13,500 both-relayed session-hours, so at small scale it costs $0.
- A $4 DigitalOcean VPS covers about 7,500 both-relayed session-hours inside its 500 GiB, then costs about $0.0007 per hour.
- At 200 relayed session-hours a month, coturn works out to about $0.02/h, against $0 for Cloudflare.
- At $0.40/GB, Twilio would cost about $0.028 per both-relayed session-hour.
- WebRTC overhead raises the relay's traffic about 65–100% above the 36 MB/h payload figure.

Before relying on Cloudflare, two things should be measured: the 10-peer-address cap per allocation, and relay latency from the testers' homes. The 2.3 home-network probe could measure both.
